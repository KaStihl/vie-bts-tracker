## Post A (airfares) - EN

> Passenger counts couldn't tell me whether low-cost airlines made tickets cheaper. So I added Eurostat's airfare price index to the report.
>
> September 2026, year over year:
> • Slovakia −55.8 %
> • Austria +12.0 %
> • EU average +5.6 %
>
> Three design decisions:
> 1. A three-month average next to the latest month. The index is noisy: Slovakia moved between −23 % and +40 % in 2025.
> 2. No red and no green. A lower price is good for the traveller and bad for the airline, so colour shouldn't judge.
> 3. The text under the chart says the index covers a country's consumer basket, not flights from one airport, and that a fall doesn't prove its cause.
>
> I checked the data against another source quoting Eurostat and they matched to a tenth of a percent.
>

## Post B (September) - EN

> In the first nine months Vienna lost 1.09 million passengers. Bratislava gained 1.84 million.
>
> September 2026:
> • Vienna 2,910,597 (−7.5 %)
> • Bratislava 498,957 (+89 %)
> • Nine months: Vienna 23.48 M (−4.5 %), Bratislava 3.69 M (+99 %)
>
> So Bratislava grew by more than Vienna lost. In a pure transfer we'd expect similar numbers. Part of the growth comes from elsewhere, but where from, these data can't say.

> A small data note: Bratislava published only a nine-month total. I derived September as the total minus January to August. Eurostat later gave the same number.
